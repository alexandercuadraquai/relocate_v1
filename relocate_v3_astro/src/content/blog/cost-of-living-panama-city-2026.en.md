**Living comfortably in Panama City in 2026 generally costs between $1,500 and $2,500 per month for a single person or couple** — well below comparable costs in most major US or Canadian cities — though your actual number depends heavily on your neighborhood, housing choice, and lifestyle. Here's an honest, realistic breakdown.

## Why Panama City Costs Less Than You'd Expect

Part of the reason Panama City is more affordable than many North American or European cities comes down to two structural factors. First, Panama's territorial tax system only taxes income generated within the country — foreign-sourced income, including pensions, dividends, and remote work earnings, is tax-exempt for residents. That doesn't lower local prices directly, but it can meaningfully increase your effective take-home budget if your income comes from abroad.

Second, Panama's dollarized, fast-growing economy keeps prices relatively predictable for anyone earning in US dollars — there's no currency exchange risk to budget around, unlike relocating to a country with a fluctuating local currency. Combined with a lower overall cost structure than the US or Canada, this is why so many retirees and remote workers find their money goes noticeably further here.

## Housing Costs: Rent vs. Buy

Housing is the single biggest variable in your monthly budget, and it varies enormously by neighborhood. Premium areas like Punta Pacifica and Costa del Este — with modern high-rises, ocean views, and walkability to upscale amenities — sit at the top of the price range. Mid-range neighborhoods like El Cangrejo and Bella Vista offer a more moderate cost while still being centrally located. Areas outside the city center tend to be the most affordable option for renters and buyers alike.

These are general estimates based on common expat reports, not an official index — rental prices move with the market, so confirm current listings with a local real estate broker before budgeting a specific number. ## Groceries & Dining Out

Grocery costs in Panama City land somewhere between US and Central American price levels — imported goods and specialty items cost more, while local produce, meat, and seafood are noticeably cheaper. Dining out ranges from inexpensive local "fondas" to international restaurants with prices comparable to a mid-size US city. As with housing, these are general estimate ranges, not an official cost index, and they're consistent with the overall $1,500–$2,500/month comfortable-living anchor for a single person or couple.

## Transportation

Panama City has a growing Metro system that provides an affordable way to get around the city without a car, along with widespread taxi and rideshare availability. Many expats choose to go car-free, especially if they live centrally, while others prefer the flexibility of owning a car for trips outside the city. Traffic in Panama City can be significant during peak hours, which is a factor worth considering when choosing your neighborhood relative to where you'll spend most of your time.

## Healthcare Costs

Panama City is home to several modern private hospitals with US-trained specialists, and many are accustomed to treating international patients. Most expats and retirees carry private health insurance rather than relying solely on out-of-pocket payments, and Pensionado Visa holders also benefit from a discount on hospital bills under Panama's retiree benefits program. Healthcare quality and availability outside the major cities is more limited, which is a factor many retirees weigh when choosing where in Panama to settle.

## Utilities & Internet

Basic utilities — electricity, water, and internet — are a smaller line item relative to housing, though air conditioning use (common in Panama's tropical climate) can noticeably affect your electricity bill depending on your unit's size and usage habits. Internet service in Panama City is generally reliable and reasonably priced compared to housing and dining costs, which matters if you're relocating as a remote worker.

## How Panama City Compares to Boquete, Coronado, and David

If Panama City's cost of living feels like the high end of your budget, it's worth knowing it generally is the higher end of Panama's overall cost spectrum. Cities and towns like Boquete, Coronado, and David all have well-established expat and retiree communities, and housing and everyday expenses in these areas are typically more affordable than in Panama City proper — while still offering the infrastructure and community support that make settling in easier. Many retirees choose Panama City for its healthcare and urban amenities, then consider a smaller town if lower costs are the priority.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much money do I need to live comfortably in Panama City?

Most retirees and expats report a comfortable lifestyle in the $1,500–$2,500/month range, though costs vary significantly by neighborhood and lifestyle choices.

### Is Panama City more expensive than the rest of Panama?

Yes, generally. Panama City — especially exclusive neighborhoods like Punta Pacifica — costs more than smaller cities and towns like Boquete, Coronado, or David, where housing and daily expenses are typically lower.

### Does Panama's territorial tax system reduce my cost of living?

Indirectly, yes — foreign-sourced income such as pensions, dividends, and remote work earnings is tax-exempt for residents under Panama's territorial tax system, which can meaningfully increase your effective take-home budget even if it doesn't change local prices.
