**The Friendly Nations Visa is Panama's most popular residency program for citizens of over 50 approved countries**, granting permanent residency and a work permit to applicants who establish an economic tie to Panama — through employment, business activity, or a qualifying bank deposit. Below, we break down exactly who qualifies, what it costs, and how the process works step by step.

## What Is the Friendly Nations Visa?

The Friendly Nations Visa is Panama's residency program designed for citizens of countries that Panama considers to have strong diplomatic, commercial, and historical ties. Unlike programs built around a large capital investment, this visa is built around demonstrating a genuine connection to the Panamanian economy — whether through a job offer, starting a business, or placing a qualifying fixed-term bank deposit.

What makes it especially attractive is the combination of benefits: it comes with an included work permit (something not every Panama residency path offers), it has no minimum income requirement, and it opens a direct path to permanent residency and, eventually, citizenship. That combination is exactly why it has become the go-to option for professionals, remote workers who want a local economic tie, entrepreneurs, and families relocating from an approved country.

As with any immigration program, the exact list of qualifying countries and the fine print of "economic tie" requirements are set by executive decree and can be updated by the Panamanian government. Our team will confirm the current requirements and country list for your specific case before you file.

## Which Countries Qualify in 2026?

The Friendly Nations list includes more than 50 countries, and it covers a wide swath of the world that most applicants come from: the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, most of the European Union (including Spain, France, Germany, and Italy), and a large number of Latin American nations such as Mexico, Argentina, Chile, and Costa Rica.

That said, the full list contains 50+ countries and is set by Panamanian executive decree, so it has been amended before and could change again. Always confirm your specific country's current status with our team before you begin gathering documents — a country that qualified a few years ago is not guaranteed to still be on the list, and new countries have occasionally been added.

## Requirements Checklist

**Citizenship of an approved Friendly Nation.** You must hold a valid passport from one of the 50+ countries on Panama's current list. This is the threshold requirement — without it, this specific visa path isn't available to you (though other Panama visas may still apply).

**An economic tie to Panama.** This is the core of the program: an employment relationship, an active business you own or operate in Panama, or a fixed-term bank deposit of at least $5,000. We cover the three options in detail below.

**A valid passport and clean police record.** Your passport needs to remain valid well beyond your application date, and you'll need a police clearance certificate from your country of residence (and, in some cases, your country of citizenship) showing no criminal history.

**Proof of professional or economic activity.** Beyond simply establishing the economic tie, you'll typically need supporting documentation — an employment contract, business registration documents, or bank deposit certificates — that demonstrates the tie is real and ongoing.

**No minimum income requirement.** Unlike the Pensionado Visa's $1,000/month pension threshold, the Friendly Nations Visa does not require you to prove a specific monthly income, which is part of why it appeals to entrepreneurs and business owners whose income may not come from a traditional pension or salary.

## The 3 Ways to Establish an "Economic Tie"

### Employment in Panama

If a Panamanian company offers you a position, that employment relationship can serve as your economic tie. This route typically requires an employment contract and proof that the employer is properly registered and operating in Panama. It's a natural fit if you're relocating for a specific job or have been offered a role by a Panama-based company.

### Starting or Owning a Business

Rather than being employed by someone else, you can establish your economic tie by forming and operating your own business in Panama — commonly a Panamanian corporation (S.A.). This path pairs naturally with our corporation formation services, since you'll need a properly registered company with evidence of real economic activity, not just a corporate shell.

### A Fixed-Term Bank Deposit

The simplest route for many applicants is a fixed-term deposit of at least $5,000 in a Panamanian bank. This option requires no business operations or employer, just the deposit itself, held for the term required under current regulations. It's often the fastest path for applicants who don't yet have a Panama-based job or business but want to qualify quickly.
